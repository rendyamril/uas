Mount Rinjani: One of the World's Best Treks

Rendy  •  Published by Green Rinjani

Mount Rinjani in Lombok, Indonesia, has earned its place as one of the best treks in the world according to Lonely Planet. The publication has consistently ranked it highly due to its challenging and rewarding trek that offers both natural beauty and cultural significance.

Why Rinjani Stands Out

1. Dramatic Volcano Views: Mount Rinjani is a massive active volcano standing at 3,726 meters (12,224 feet), and the views from the summit are truly spectacular. Trekkers are treated to a panoramic view of the Segara Anak Lake, a crater lake that sits inside the caldera, along with lush forests, cliffs, and the distant Indian Ocean.

2. Segara Anak Lake: The lake at the summit is one of the main attractions of the trek. It is surrounded by volcanic rock and is considered sacred by the local Sasak people. Many trekkers spend a night camping by the lake, making it a perfect rest stop before making the final push to the summit.

3. Diverse Terrain: The trek to the summit of Rinjani is not just about the volcano but also about the varied terrain. Hikers pass through dense tropical forests, savannahs, and mountain ridges. The range of ecosystems creates a constantly changing experience.

4. Spiritual Significance: For the indigenous Sasak people, Rinjani is more than just a mountain. It is a sacred site, and many locals perform rituals at the summit or the crater lake. This cultural significance adds an extra layer of reverence to the trekking experience.

The Legend of Dewi Anjani

Local people of Lombok believe that the peak of Mount Rinjani is the invisible palace of Queen Dewi Anjani. To the southeast from the peak, there is a dusty and barren area called “Segara Muncar”. This is reportedly the invisible palace of Queen Dewi Anjani and her followers, the good spirits. According to ancient Lombok legend, Dewi Anjani is the daughter of a king who did not allow her to marry her boyfriend. At a spring on Lombok called Mandala, she disappeared and mysteriously transitioned from the real world to the spirit world.

Rinjani National Park Facts

Mount Rinjani is an active volcano in Indonesia on the island of Lombok. It rises to 3,726 meters (12,224 ft), making it the second-highest volcano in Indonesia. Adjacent to the volcano is a 6-by-8.5-kilometer caldera, which is filled partially by the crater lake known as Segara Anak (child of the sea) because its water is as blue as the sea. UNESCO made Mount Rinjani a part of the Global Geoparks Network in April 2018.

Planning Your Trek

Best Time to Climb: The best months to trek Mount Rinjani are generally from April to October, avoiding the rainy season. The National Park closes from the 1st of January to March due to heavy rains.

Temperature: The temperature at the summit can drop to freezing (0°C – 3°C). At the crater rim, it's around 8-10°C at night.

Safety: It is safe to climb as long as you listen to your mountain guide’s instructions and stay on the marked trail.

Popular Routes: After the 2018 earthquake, several routes were repaired. The three recommended trekking routes are Sembalun, Senaru, and Torean.

The Sacred Lake and Springs

Segara Anak Lake: The lake is so wide, it looks like the ocean. It holds various mysteries and invisible powers. It is forbidden to have sexual intercourse, complain, or say disrespectful things in this sacred area.

Healing Hot Springs: The hot spring Aiq Kalak means “hot water”, and it is used to cure various diseases. In ancient times, weapons were tested in the spring for magic powers.

The Caves of Lombok

There are three famous caves on Lombok: Goa Susu, Goa Payung, and Dan Goa Manik. Susu Cave is a good place for self-reflection and meditation. It is said that people with sinful minds will have difficulty entering its narrow entrance, while those with pure minds will enter easily.
